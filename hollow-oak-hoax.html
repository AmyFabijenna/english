This is the skill the story is really about. Anybody can build a website that looks official in an afternoon. Learning to look at a page and ask the right questions is one of the most useful things you will ever learn, in English or in any language.

Six questions worth asking about any page

  1. Who wrote this? Is there a real name, and does that person have any reason to know about the subject? "Admin", "Staff Writer" and no name at all are all warning signs.
  2. When was it published? A page with no date could be from this morning or from 2011. For anything that changes, the date matters.
  3. What is the address telling me? Look at the domain carefully. Real organisations rarely use long, hyphen-filled names, and a lookalike domain is a classic trick.
  4. Where did the information come from? Does the page link to a study, a document, a named expert? If a claim has no source you can follow, you only have somebody's word for it.
  5. Does anybody else report this? If something genuinely remarkable has happened, you will usually find it in more than one independent place.
  6. What does this page want from me? Urgency, outrage, "share before it's deleted", a shop button: all of these tell you the page has a purpose beyond informing you.
